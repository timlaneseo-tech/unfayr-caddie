How to Write a No-Show Fee Policy

A no-show fee is a charge for an appointment a client books and then neither attends nor cancels in time. It exists because the time was reserved and could have gone to someone else. Most clients accept the idea without complaint, as long as they knew about it before they booked.

Why Have One

Partly to recover revenue, but mostly to change behavior. Businesses that introduce a no-show fee see no-shows fall even when they rarely charge it. The fee makes the appointment feel like a commitment rather than a placeholder.

Example Policies

Salon: "Appointments missed without 24 hours' notice are charged a $25 no-show fee to the card on file."

Dental or medical practice: "We require 24 hours' notice to cancel or reschedule. Missed appointments are subject to a $50 fee. Repeated no-shows may result in dismissal from the practice."

Therapist: "Sessions cancelled with less than 24 hours' notice, or not attended, are charged the full session fee, as insurance does not cover missed sessions."

Massage or spa: "A 50% deposit is taken at booking and is forfeited for no-shows or cancellations within 48 hours."

Setting the Amount

The fee should sting a little without feeling punitive. Common choices are a flat $25 to $50, half the service price, or the full price for long or specialist sessions. If you take deposits, the deposit usually is the fee.

Making It Stick

Three things make a no-show fee collectable: the client saw it before booking, the client agreed to it, and you have a way to charge it. A checkbox on the booking page covers the first two. A card on file covers the third. Without a card, the fee becomes an invoice most people ignore, so the practical rule is to require a card for any booking the fee applies to. Whether you are allowed to charge it is rarely the issue; it is a term the client agreed to, like any other price. What matters is being able to show they agreed.

Communicating It

State it once on the booking page, once in the confirmation, and in a short line in the reminder text. When a no-show happens, send a plain message the same day: "We missed you today. Our no-show fee of $25 has been charged as per our policy. We'd love to see you again; here is a link to rebook."

Waiving It

Waive it for genuine emergencies and for the first offense from a regular, and say that you are waiving it. The goodwill is worth more than the fee, and the client now knows the policy is real.
