How to Reduce No-Shows: 11 Tactics That Work

Every empty chair is paid for twice: once in the revenue that did not arrive and again in the client who could have had the slot. Across Slotwise accounts, businesses that use none of the tactics below lose around one booking in five to no-shows. Businesses that use most of them lose around one in twenty.

1. Send a Reminder the Day Before

The single biggest lever. A text 24 hours ahead cuts no-shows by about a third on its own. Email alone does far less; people do not check email on the way to a haircut.

2. Send a Second Reminder Two Hours Before

The day-before text catches people who forgot. The two-hour text catches people who remembered and then got distracted. Together they confirm roughly 80 percent of bookings.

3. Ask for a One-Letter Confirmation

"Reply C to confirm" turns a passive reminder into a tiny commitment. Clients who confirm show up far more reliably than clients who merely received the text, and clients who do not confirm can be called.

4. Call the Unconfirmed

Anyone who has not confirmed by the morning of the appointment gets a call. Half will confirm on the spot, a quarter will reschedule, and you learn about the rest with hours to fill the slot instead of minutes.

5. Take a Card on File or a Deposit

Nothing focuses attention like a financial stake. A card on file with a stated no-show fee is enough for most service businesses. High-value or long appointments justify an actual deposit.

6. Write Down a Cancellation Policy

And show it before booking. Clients who agreed to a policy cancel earlier and no-show less, even when the fee is never charged.

7. Shorten the Booking Horizon

Appointments booked three weeks out no-show at twice the rate of appointments booked three days out. If your calendar allows it, hold some near-term capacity rather than filling every slot far ahead.

8. Make Rescheduling Easy

A client who cannot make it will either reschedule or vanish. If rescheduling means a phone call during business hours, more vanish. Put a reschedule link in every reminder.

9. Run a Waitlist

When a cancellation does happen, a waitlist turns it into a text: "A spot just opened at 2pm today, want it?" The first person to reply gets the slot and the loss disappears.

10. Track Who No-Shows

Most no-shows come from a small group of repeat offenders. Flag them, require a deposit from them specifically, or book them only in slots that are easy to refill.

11. Follow Up After a No-Show

A kind text the same day ("We missed you, want to rebook?") recovers a surprising share of them, and it reminds the client that someone noticed.

Do These Together

None of these is magic alone. Reminders plus confirmation plus a card on file is the combination that moves a business from one-in-five to one-in-twenty. Slotwise does the first nine automatically on every plan, including the free one.
