Cancellation Policy Template

A cancellation policy is the sentence or two that turns "I'll try to make it" into a commitment. Without one, every booking is a maybe. With one, clients think twice before letting a slot go to waste, and when they do cancel late you have something to point to.

Most small businesses put off writing one because it feels confrontational. It does not have to be. The template below is firm, polite, and short enough to fit on a booking page, in a confirmation text, and on a sign by the register.

The Template

We understand that plans change. If you need to cancel or reschedule, please let us know at least 24 hours before your appointment. Cancellations with less than 24 hours' notice, and missed appointments, are charged 50% of the booked service. Thank you for respecting our time and the time of clients waiting for an opening.

Swap 24 hours for 48 if your services are long or your schedule fills far ahead. Swap 50% for a flat fee if your prices vary a lot. Keep the thank-you; it changes the tone from a threat to a request.

Variations

Salons and barbers often use a 24-hour window with a flat fee, because a missed cut is a fixed loss. Massage therapists and med spas tend toward 48 hours with a percentage, because sessions are long and deposits are common. Therapists usually follow the standard their licensing board or insurers expect, which is often 24 hours and a full session fee for no-shows. Photographers work on contracts with deposits and a longer window, since a shoot date is hard to resell.

Where to Put It

On the booking page, above the confirm button, with a checkbox if your booking software supports one. In the confirmation message. In the reminder text, in one short line. On a sign at the front desk. The more places a client sees it before the appointment, the less awkward it is to apply afterward.

Applying It

Decide in advance what you will do the first time a long-standing client cancels late. Most businesses waive the first offense and say so: "I've waived the fee this time; just a heads up that it applies going forward." That keeps the relationship and makes the second time easy. Charging a card on file is simplest, which is one reason booking tools ask for one; otherwise add the fee to the next visit.

If you have ever wondered whether a policy like this will hold up, the practical answer is that it does when the client agreed to it before booking. A checkbox or a clear notice on the booking page is what makes it enforceable in practice; a sign they never saw is not.
