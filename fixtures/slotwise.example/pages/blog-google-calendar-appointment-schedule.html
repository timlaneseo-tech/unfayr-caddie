How to Use Google Calendar Appointment Schedules

Google Calendar has a built-in way to let people book time with you. It is called an appointment schedule, it is free with a personal Google account, and for a lot of simple cases it is all you need. Here is how to set one up, and where it starts to creak.

Setting One Up

Open Google Calendar on the web, click Create, and choose Appointment schedule. Give it a name, set the appointment length, choose the days and hours you are available, and pick which calendar to check for conflicts. Save, and Google gives you a booking page link to share.

Sharing the Booking Page

Copy the link from the schedule's settings and put it wherever people reach you. Google also offers an embed snippet for a website. People who book get a calendar invitation and an email confirmation.

What It Does Well

It is free, it checks your real calendar for conflicts, and it sends confirmations. For a consultant booking calls or a teacher offering office hours, that is the whole job.

Where It Falls Short

Appointment schedules on a free account offer one booking page and no payments. Paid Google Workspace plans add multiple schedules and can take payments through Stripe, but there are still no text reminders, no deposits or no-show fees, no staff-level calendars, no service menu with prices, and no waitlist. For a business that lives on appointments, those gaps are where no-shows and double bookings come from.

Google Calendar vs. a Scheduling Tool

If you book a handful of calls a week, use Google. If clients are paying you for the time, a scheduling tool that sends text reminders and holds a card will pay for itself with the first no-show it prevents.
