What Counts as an Emergency

Anything that is actively damaging your home or making it unsafe: a burst or frozen pipe, water pouring from a ceiling, sewage backing up into a tub or floor drain, no water at all, a water heater leaking from the tank, or the smell of gas near a water heater or furnace. If you smell gas, leave the house first and call the gas company, then call us.

A dripping faucet, a slow drain, or a running toilet can wait until morning, and we will tell you that honestly on the phone so you do not pay an after-hours premium for something that is not urgent.

Stop the Water First

Your home's main water shut-off valve is usually where the water line enters the house: in the garage, a basement or crawlspace wall, a utility closet, or outside near the foundation on the street side. In many Boise homes it is a round or lever handle near the water heater or pressure regulator. Turn a round handle clockwise until it stops; turn a lever a quarter turn so it sits across the pipe. If you cannot find it, the meter box at the curb has a shut-off too, though it may need a meter key.

For a burst pipe, shut off the main, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the lines, and move anything you can out of the wet area. For frozen pipes that have not yet burst, open the faucet they feed and warm the pipe gradually with a hair dryer; never use an open flame.
